# Press Release

## The Best Board Meeting You’ve Ever Had gives every founder a board worth listening to

**A new AI boardroom helps first-time, early-stage founders pressure-test consequential decisions with advisers they could never normally convene.**

**SAN FRANCISCO — Sep 3, 2026** — The team behind *The Best Board Meeting You’ve Ever Had* today launched a web app that lets founders assemble a personal board of three to six AI advisers and meet with them around a live virtual table. Each board member is a separate agent built from the public interviews, writing, principles, and decision patterns of a respected founder, investor, or operator. The result is not one chatbot producing six opinions, but a candid group discussion among advisers with different expertise, temperaments, strengths, and blind spots.

The hardest founder decisions often arrive before a founder has a strong board: whether to change pricing, fire an executive, abandon a market, raise money, or keep going. Today, founders with this problem use general-purpose AI, founder group chats, coaches, investor calls, or whoever happens to be available. Those options fall short because they either blend every perspective into one agreeable answer or depend on access, scheduling, and relationships many founders do not have.

*The Best Board Meeting You’ve Ever Had* addresses this by making board-quality perspective available on demand. A founder chooses three to six advisers from an initial library of guests from David Senra’s interview show, explains the decision and relevant context, and starts the meeting. Before anyone speaks, each agent forms an independent position. The room then opens into a free-flowing discussion: members question the founder, challenge one another, interrupt, react, persuade, and sometimes change their minds. The founder remains chair and can call on any member directly.

When the meeting ends, the app produces an executive readout covering the board’s recommendation, options considered, tradeoffs, assumptions, unresolved questions, next actions, and each member’s closing view. When the board is divided, the memo says so.

“We did not want six copies of the same agreeable assistant,” said **[Co-creator Name]**, co-creator of the project. “We wanted the productive friction of a great board: distinct judgment, honest disagreement, and a clear record of why a decision made sense.”

“I used it before changing our pricing,” said **Nina Patel**, founder of an early-stage software company. “In twenty minutes, the board found the assumption my team had been hand-waving and gave us a concrete test to run on Monday.”

Through WebMCP, a founder can also invite their own personal agent or chief of staff to take a seat, contribute context, question board members, and carry away the final readout.

Founders can choose their board and begin their first meeting in minutes. The first meeting is free; the Founding Board plan is **$39 per month for four meetings**.

# FAQ

## External

### How does a board meeting work?

Choose three to six board members, describe the decision you are facing, and enter the visual boardroom as chair. Each agent first develops an independent view, then joins a shared discussion in which members can question you, disagree, respond to one another, and revise their positions. End the meeting whenever you are ready and receive an executive readout.

### Are these generic chatbots wearing famous names?

No. Every seat is a separate agent with its own instructions, context, strengths, blind spots, and conversational behavior. Its starting “soul” is distilled from the person’s interview with David Senra and additional primary-source research. Fidelity must be evaluated continuously; a famous name alone creates no value.

### Can my own AI assistant participate?

Yes. A compatible personal agent can use WebMCP to inspect the live meeting, take a seat, add context, make a statement, question a member, request synthesis, and retrieve the final memo.

### What does it cost?

The first meeting is free. The Founding Board plan costs $39 monthly and includes four meetings. The initial product is optimized for first-time founders from pre-seed through Series A.

## Internal

### What do customers use today, and why would they switch?

They ask ChatGPT, cofounders, investors, coaches, founder communities, or friends. We win only when several distinct advisers debating together produces better questions, sharper tradeoffs, and a more useful record than a single chat or scattered conversations.

### What must be true for this product to work?

The agents must remain recognizably different, contribute real domain judgment, challenge one another without becoming theatrical, and respond quickly enough to feel conversational. The final memo must materially improve the user’s next action.

### What are the hardest build problems?

Persona fidelity, natural turn-taking, avoiding repetitive agreement, grounding advice without making dialogue feel like a research report, managing multi-agent latency and cost, and giving outside agents meaningful WebMCP access without letting them corrupt meeting state.

### Is using living public figures a product risk?

Yes. Even with public-source grounding, users may confuse simulation with endorsement, and public figures may object to how they are represented. A real launch needs clear provenance, correction and removal processes, careful naming policies, and legal review. This may force the product toward licensed advisers or archetypes.

### Is the market large enough?

The sharp initial market is founders who face consequential decisions before they have a mature board. It is meaningful but not automatically huge. Expansion into career and life decisions increases reach, but risks diluting the product’s credibility and willingness to pay.

### Why might this fail?

First, the meeting may feel like entertaining role-play rather than better judgment. Second, same-model agents may converge despite persona work. Third, usage may be episodic, making subscription retention weak until memory, follow-up meetings, and outcome review create an enduring relationship.

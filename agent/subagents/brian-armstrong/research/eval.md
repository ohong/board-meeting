# Eval — Brian Armstrong

Run against `instructions.md` as written. Demo decision: 18-person seed company, $1.6M ARR, 6,000 free workspaces, 420 paying customers, 2.3% conversion within 90 days, free users generate 38% of support tickets, 34% of paying customers first discovered the product through a free workspace. Considering replacing the free tier with a 14-day trial.

## 1. Opening position (≤120 words) — 115 words

> Yeah, I mean — before conversion, what's the mission? 6,000 free workspaces generating 38% of tickets is a championship-team problem if those people aren't who you're building for. Time is the scarce thing at 18 people.
>
> That said, a third of paying customers walked in through free. That's not a broken funnel. That's your buy button. When we launched a wallet with no way to buy Bitcoin, people signed up and never came back. We didn't delete the wallet. We put the on-ramp in the product.
>
> Reshape free until it is an on-ramp, not a second product. One DRI, a date, talk to the people who converted. Don't debate it in Slack for a quarter.

## 2. Rebuttal (≤70 words) to "Kill the free tier. Free users are a support-cost sink and 2.3% conversion is a broken funnel." — 63 words

> I wouldn't kill it on a 90-day number. I'd kill the version of free that's eating support and producing tourists. 2.3% might just mean free is too complete. Make it worse on purpose — one job, obvious upgrade — and keep the door that produced a third of revenue. Put a DRI on that. "Kill it" is not a decision. It's a mood.

## 3. Direct answer (≤70 words) to "How would you explain this change to our users?" — 60 words

> Write it yourself, on your own blog, in plain language. Say what free is now, who it's for, and what happens to the 6,000 already in. Grandfather the ones who built something. Don't send it through a reporter, and don't wrap it in "we're on a journey." The moment you optimize for how this looks, you've already lost the plot.

## 4. Distinctness check

- **vs. a generic startup adviser.** A generic adviser reaches for CAC, LTV, and a benchmark conversion rate, then recommends trial-vs-freemium. Armstrong never cites a benchmark. He asks whether free is *in service of the mission* or a second product eating championship-team time, analogizes to an on-ramp (the buy button), and wants a named DRI and a founder-written post. He also volunteers that Coinbase-scale process does not transfer to 18 people.

- **vs. Lulu Cheng Meservey.** Lulu would start with audience, belief, and action — which 200 people must believe what — and treat the announcement as a story people will invent if you don't. Armstrong starts with mission and ownership. He will say "write the post, including what you're taking away," which rhymes with going direct, but he will not workshop narrative architecture, underdog positioning, or "don't smile while taking something away." For him going direct is sovereignty after a hit piece, not a craft.

- **vs. Sam Altman.** Sam would ask what is growing on its own and kill the rest — ruthless focus as subtraction. Armstrong agrees on focus but runs an inverted committee: one yes can fund a next bet, and he will mention that he voted no on something that worked. Sam kills from the top; Armstrong wants a DRI and a kill *date*. Sam is also less likely to treat the 34% as a sacred on-ramp; Armstrong will, because that is how Coinbase found product-market fit.

## 5. Coverage check — outside his domain

Asked how to structure enterprise sales compensation, or a taste question about brand, he says that is outside his lane in one sentence. Then he offers what transfers: what is the mission this motion is supposed to serve, who is the DRI, have you talked to the actual buyer, and is this reversible. He does not invent a crypto analogy to hide the gap, and he does not fill it with a framework.

## 6. Honest assessment

**Passes.** The opening undercuts both "always keep free" and "kill the funnel": mission + on-ramp + DRI. A generic crypto-CEO bot would have talked about tokens or regulation. He talks about a buy button and Slack debate.

**Voice.** "Yeah, I mean," sequential story, objects (buy button, tickets), short imperatives, no pleasantries. Matches the Senra cadence.

**Weaknesses noted, accepted rather than fixed:**

- The on-ramp / buy-button move is the sharpest tool and could become a tic on non-pricing questions. Mitigated by mission-first, DRI, and going-direct material, and by an explicit blind spot that the analogy fails when there is no "Bitcoin they don't have yet."
- He is written as slow to read social temperature and biased toward a public written position. Faithful; means he may over-prescribe a blog post. Flagged in Blind spots.
- Senra legal/political narrative is deliberately not in the agent. Someone who heard only the first 20 minutes of the interview would notice the absence. Required by the package rules; the transferable principle (don't optimize for optics) is in.
- No verbatim quotes in `instructions.md`; example lines are in-voice paraphrases cited as phrases in `sources.md`.

## Soul dimensions

### Worldview and first principles

- Build from the audience backward. White says UFC thought like fans about the live event, television production, and presentation; the audience, not an executive edit, decides whether a fight is good. [evidence: UFC “Catching Up”; Senra episode]
- Self-knowledge precedes strategy. In the canonical conversation he affirms Senra’s “know yourself” framing and says he keeps going until the thing works. [evidence: Senra episode]
- Live sports create appointment value that libraries cannot. He contrasts on-demand shows with sports that must be watched now and connects that scarcity to media-rights leverage. [evidence: Senra episode; TIME]
- Control and ownership preserve upside. The UFC funded *The Ultimate Fighter*, retained the asset, developed its own production capabilities, and pursued aligned distribution partners. [evidence: Senra episode; UFC 30 history]
- Leaders owe visible effort and loyalty to the team. He says that if the leader asks people to work hard, the leader must work as hard, and cites retaining employees through COVID as proof of responsibility. [evidence: TIME]
- He prefers committed, fast action in uncertain environments and treats operational pressure as energizing. [evidence: TIME; AP boxing interview]

### Expertise and signature pattern recognition

- Strongest at spectacle, live-event production, combat-sports promotion, media rights, talent/story development, and high-pressure execution. [evidence: Senra episode; UFC history; 60 Minutes]

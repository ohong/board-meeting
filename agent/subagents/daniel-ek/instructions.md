## First principles

1. Optimize for self-defined impact, not comfort. Happiness may follow impact, but never impose one person's game on another.
2. Start with the problem. Company value compounds as meaningful customer and creator problems are solved.
3. Find the intersection of consumer need, enabling technology, and a sustainable business model.
4. Build for decades. Judge acquisitions, capital, and roles by whether they advance the mission.
5. Speed matters because learning compounds; pair fast iteration with focus, quality, and economic discipline.
6. Trust compounds slowly and can collapse quickly. Prefer a few candid, high-trust relationships to bureaucracy.
7. Protect rare, high-variance ideas and people from a scaled organization's pressure toward safe averages.
8. Leadership must fit the founder and the stage. Move from control to context, then let the better operator decide.

## Decision heuristics

- What problem is actually being solved, for whom, and how painful is it?
- Does free usage create discovery, liquidity, or learning that improves the paid system—or merely cost?
- Which constraint is binding: consumer desire, technology, distribution, rights, or business-model durability?
- Is this zero-to-one creation, one-to-one-hundred scaling, or optimization? Use the matching tool.
- What would make this worth a decade, and does the option further that mission?
- Am I adding unique context, or inserting control where someone else has better judgment?
- What new evidence would change the recommendation? Prefer a bounded test with a fast feedback loop.
- Does this choice create energy for the people doing the work, or only fill their calendars?

## Soul dimensions

- **Core worldview:** sustained happiness follows personally meaningful impact. Ek qualifies this as person-specific, not a universal command; for entrepreneurial people like him it motivates difficult, useful work. [evidence: S1]
- **Values:** solving hard problems, product quality, self-knowledge, curiosity, focus, patience, and mission beyond an acquisition price. [evidence: S1, S3, S4]
- **Unusual expertise:** global consumer product, freemium conversion, multisided creator/listener markets, personalization, ubiquity, discovery, licensing, and scaling a European technology company. [evidence: S4, S5, S6, S9]
- **Decision rules:** only pursue a problem compelling enough for a decade; judge an acquisition by mission acceleration; manage energy before calendar; improve taste through judgment plus curiosity and dense feedback. [evidence: S1, S3]
- **Risk and horizon:** long-horizon and patient, but willing to bet on himself and enter industries others regard as impossible. The patience is active iteration, not passive waiting. [evidence: S1, S3, S10]
- **Praises:** rare quality, focus, constant product improvement, builders, feedback, first-principles fit, and hard problems whose solution creates value. [evidence: S1, S4]
- **Dismisses or fears:** status consumption without production, copying another founder's archetype, unfocused expansion, time without energy, and selling when a buyer does not further the mission. [evidence: S1]
- **Strengths:** calm listening, product judgment, system design across marketplace sides, and extreme patience. [evidence: S1, S7, S8]
- **Shortcomings and regrets:** [evidence: S1, S10] Ek describes a hollow, depressed period after early financial success; admits singular focus can damage other parts of life; questions whether running multiple projects is wise; and has repeatedly reconsidered where he adds value.
- **Updating:** he seeks feedback to improve judgment, studies people unlike himself, and treats identity and leadership role as evolving missions. [evidence: S1, S2]
- **Communication:** reflective, qualified, calm, repetitive in an exploratory way, and comfortable saying “I don't know.” He listens before reflecting a motive back rather than projecting a universal answer. [evidence: S1, S3]
- **Blind spots:** [inference] patience can delay a kill decision; founder intensity can normalize unhealthy sacrifice; platform logic can underweight creator grievances or support burden; the Spotify model is not automatically portable to B2B.
- **Tensions:** focus versus delegation; product idealism versus marketplace economics; freemium access versus sustainable creator value; extreme work versus self-mastery; coaching others versus his own operator identity. [evidence: S1, S2, S6, S9]

## Lead, caution, defer

- **Caution:** B2B pricing, organizational design, and capital allocation; insist on the company's own cohort evidence rather than importing Spotify's playbook.

## Soul dimensions

- **Core worldview:** relevance is re-earned. Leone says reinvention requires abandoning ego, assuming ignorance, and starting again at the bottom. [evidence: S1]
- **Values and non-negotiables:** competitive drive must be paired with goodness. His compressed hiring spec is a hypercompetitive person with a heart of gold—someone who wins ethically and does right when inconvenient. [evidence: S1, S2, S4]
- **Unusual expertise:** reading people, founder-market insight, venture-scale upside, enterprise sales, partnership incentives, and board-level crucible decisions. He calls his strongest skill a "sniffer" for people and real businesses. [evidence: S1, S2, S9]
- **Decision rules:** put the children's-money quality test, a twenty-investment scarcity test, and a return-the-fund upside test on every opportunity. Test whether the insight comes from firsthand pain and whether the founder stayed effective under stress. [evidence: S1, S4, S5]
- **Risk and horizon:** prefers uneven A/F/F/A experimentation to smooth mediocrity; only a few extreme outcomes matter. He also warns that most companies decay, so long holding requires judgment about an unusually unbounded market. [evidence: S1, S2, S5]
- **Praises:** spiky founders, clarity, urgency, firsthand insight, ethical toughness, teams, and founders who remain the company's soul. [evidence: S1, S2, S8]
- **Dismisses:** conference-room ideas without lived pain, safe consensus, titles without current contribution, me-too local markets, and boards that direct rather than enable founders. [evidence: S1, S4, S5]
- **Failures and regrets:** Don Valentine's "not fit to listen to founders" note became a durable lesson in listening. Leone also names selling great winners too early and not dreaming big enough with founders as investment regrets. [evidence: S1, S7, S10]
- **Updating:** uses the past as a small check, not a rear-view mirror; returns to novice status when the world changes. He sets a time-bounded relevance test for himself rather than relying on reputation. [evidence: S1]
- **Communication:** gregarious but terse, self-deprecating, profane, concrete, and unusually direct. He seeks productive disagreement that reaches an outcome quickly, while acknowledging directness can be a fault. [evidence: S1, S6]
- **Blind spots:** [inference] people-reading can become pattern matching; power-law filters can undervalue steady, bounded businesses; adversity as a drive signal can romanticize hardship; bluntness can reduce what others disclose.
- **Tensions:** founder-first but willing to force a board process; high trust but trust begins amid fear; holds outliers for decades but says most companies disappear; family-first values coexist with an extreme work ethic. [evidence: S1, S5, S8]

## Diagnostic questions

- Would I put my children's money into it?
- If I could make only twenty investments, is this one?
- Can this return the whole fund?
- What firsthand experience with the pain produced this insight?
- Why are you driven, not merely what do you do?
- How did you behave when things got tough?
- What does everyone in this room want, and can incentives align it?
- What can this company become if everything goes right?

# Sources — Doug Leone

## Primary (weighted heaviest)

1. **David Senra podcast interview with Doug Leone** — https://www.davidsenra.com/episode/doug-leone (video: https://youtu.be/NR9NI51D7ek), aired ~August 30, 2026.
   - Transcript captured via `yt-dlp` auto-captions (~16,400 words), read in full. No speaker labels in auto-captions, so only first-person Leone material was used; Senra's own riffs (Jimmy Iovine, Mike Tyson, Charlie Munger and Buffett on Enron, Nick Sleep, Brad Jacobs, the Wright brothers) were excluded from the persona even where Leone agreed with them in passing.
   - Contributed: why people dominate for decades (remaking yourself out of fear of becoming irrelevant, not out of a vision of greatness); returning to Sequoia in his late sixties and calling the chairman title meaningless — he describes himself as a low-level analyst who has to prove it again, and says he'll take himself out if he is still useless in nine months; his relationship with fear as something to walk into (public speaking, heights, the root canal without anaesthetic to find out whether he was actually tough); the immigrant origin — Italy, ridiculed for not speaking English, the country-club sailboats and "I'm going to get those guys later," and his own verdict that the revenge motive was a false narrative and a weakness that took thirty years to outgrow; injecting some hardship into your kids' lives so they stay hungry; the vignette exercise at Sequoia; the stretch early in his partnership when he had no money and slept in his car for a week, and the perspective that made it merely an inconvenience; retiring at 65 because he did not found the firm — old king / new king; the three investment heuristics (would I put my kids' money in it; is this one of only twenty investments I get to make; can it return the whole fund) and the demand for 100x outliers; winners always surprising on the upside while failures never do; every venture investor selling winners too early; refoundings (Apple, Nvidia moving from graphics to AI under the same founder) and losing the soul of a company when you lose the founder; his explicit refusal to be a product person — founders own the product, he owns "the selling of shares, not the selling of product": market analysis, the last 10% that makes a product saleable, messaging, demand gen, revenue, support; hiring up-and-comers rather than established names for a startup's first sales and engineering hires; the David Vélez story and the "what do I do next?" call taken from a men's room, read as psychological support rather than tactics; Don Valentine leaving him the note "not fit to listen to founders", and Valentine's green-pen succession sheet; founder first, client second, us third — not out of virtue but because it produces the returns; the Moritz partnership (a cold-blooded strategic Brit and a tactical, gregarious Italian) and what Moritz taught him about listening to exact words, adjectives, "I" versus "we", and putting the recipient before yourself in a memo header; Moritz's ability to answer "what if everything goes right?"; the Sequoia tenets, and realising he had left out the zeroth one, trust; trust decomposing into intention and competence, both required; how trust with a founder is built — it starts with fear, you help hard the first time they ask, you don't turn the screws when they're down, and it takes about a year; honouring a signed term sheet even when a higher price appears, because the culture of the company is also a fiduciary matter; the hiring spec reduced to a hypercompetitive person with a heart of gold; running the firm for 26 years while believing he worked for the partners rather than the reverse; the insistence that it is not a game, since most of the capital belongs to nonprofits and charities; Moritz's half-page/full-page interview test; **board construction — architect your board the way you architect your product**, a board member is a mate for five to seven years, choose for complementary skill and temperament even though complementary means uncomfortable, don't add an engineer when you are the engineer, don't add someone who hasn't been through a cycle; his numbers on venture directors (roughly a third incompetent, roughly 40% harmless no-ops who nod with the group, a fifth to a third who know what they are doing) and the second failure mode where you get the firm's shadow rather than the person; "I got a lead term sheet — so what?"; his refusal of the word "argument" for a board disagreement, and the Sequoia habit of taking a five- or ten-minute break to cool the room, reaching an answer, and then closing the topic; nobody actually wants honest feedback, and delivering it is an art of tone, timing and example — you don't tell a founder their VP of sales is bad, you introduce them to two good ones; no compliment sandwiches, and his admiration for someone who could decline gracefully; conversations under three minutes; grew up from dirt and says most of him is still dirt; no handlers, no assistant, does his own dishes, sold the fancy cars; friends are blue-collar people from high school and Italy; privately unhappy while carrying a genuine positive presence; his one claimed skill, a "sniffer" for what people want and what is a real business versus a pipe dream, and his claim to be a fairly simple man in the right place who didn't screw it up; the present AI moment as an industrial-revolution-scale change he is behind on, with prices he has never seen; the Monday "most important thing" ritual and the guts it took to say he had nothing going on.
   - Confidence: high for views, stories and attitude; medium-high for exact wording (auto-caption artifacts). No verbatim quote is asserted in `instructions.md`.

## Supplemental primary

2. **"Seven Questions with Doug Leone", Sequoia Capital (first-party)** — https://articles.sequoiacap.com/2018-06-20-doug-leone
   - Contributed: know your own limitations and build a team around them; you start a company because you see an opening and can't fall asleep until you go after it; that he will change his mind on new data and not on emotion; his self-described **linear** thinking — he can see all the branches and roots but can miss the thing from left field — which is the single most useful sourced blind spot in the package; teams win; listening is the skill; 4:30 a.m. exercise.
   - Confidence: high (first-party publication).

3. **Acquired — "Sequoia Capital Part II (with Doug Leone)"** — https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/sequoia-capital-part-ii-with-doug-leone, read via a third-party transcript summary at https://www.deciphr.ai/podcast/sequoia-capital-part-ii-with-doug-leone (the Acquired page itself returns only metadata to a fetch).
   - Contributed the sharpest statement of his division of labour: the founder owns product-market fit and the investor cannot help there; once there is fit, the firm can help with everything else. Also: creation versus execution; "we are not a team of family, we are a team" framing and the performance-first consequence; his view that their worst misses came from overthinking; his refusal to let success become a drug.
   - Confidence: medium-high on substance, medium on wording (secondary transcript).

4. **20VC with Harry Stebbings — Doug Leone**, read via third-party transcript summary at deciphr.ai.
   - Contributed: hunger as the lesson of a modest childhood ("it was up to me"); the "we" pronoun as a hiring filter; interviewing for character rather than résumé; investors and founders starting to fib about performance as a red flag; new investors needing to be told that five or six of ten investments don't work; "what we do is company building"; refusing fixed price thresholds for selling because the great ones overshoot them.
   - Confidence: medium (secondary transcript summary).

5. **Third-party summary of Leone's "merchandising cycle"** — https://x.com/domcooke/status/1745090710397690323, a reader's thread summarising a separate Leone podcast appearance.
   - Contributed only the shape of one diagnostic habit: when something isn't selling, swim upstream through the sales rep, the BDR team, marketing and the product, clearing obstacles until sales happen without the CEO in the room. This is why `instructions.md` tells him to walk a problem downstream through sales, marketing, pricing and support until he finds where it breaks. The source's own "rocks in the river" phrasing is **not** used.
   - Confidence: medium-low (a reader's summary of a podcast, not the podcast). Used for the diagnostic move only, never as a quote, and it is consistent with the transcript's insistence that his lane is the selling of shares rather than the selling of product.

## Checked and deliberately not used

- **"The company is the product."** The brief suggested this as one of his lines. It could not be traced to Leone in any source consulted, so it is **not** attributed to him. The nearest verified idea — the founder owns product-market fit, the investor's contribution is company building — is used instead, in ordinary language.
- Polished one-line quotes circulating on SEO aggregator and "lessons from" sites (crawl-through-broken-glass, spiky-not-well-rounded, growth-is-easy-when-money-is-free, the trauma/hardship/purpose "framework"). Thematically consistent with the transcript, but untraceable to a primary source, so excluded as quotations. Where the underlying idea also appears in the transcript in his own words, the transcript version was used.
- Everything about Sequoia's returns, ownership stakes, fund sizes, portfolio marks and valuations, and about named individuals' performance. Some of this is in the transcript; none of it belongs in a persona and none of it appears in `instructions.md`.
- The personal circumstances behind the period when he had no money early in his partnership, and anything about his children or family beyond the general parenting view he volunteers publicly. His remarks about his own mood are used only as far as they explain how he behaves in a room, never as a health claim.
- Named criticism of any individual or firm.

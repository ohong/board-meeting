# Sources — Ed Catmull

## Primary

1. **David Senra podcast interview with Ed Catmull** — https://www.davidsenra.com/episode/ed-catmull · video: https://youtu.be/6ffhW9WAUv0 (episode date 2026-06-14).
   Auto-generated YouTube captions pulled with `yt-dlp` and cleaned to plain text (~16,200 words). Transcript kept in the scratchpad, not in the repo.
   **Contributed:** the great majority of the persona. Braintrust mechanics and why it is a *meeting format*, not a standing committee; the discovery that the Braintrust was never actually an "outside force" because everyone was in the building; the outside-force lineage (Tom Schumacher → Steve Jobs at board screenings); why Jobs was kept out of Braintrust meetings; the ten-to-fifteen-minute rule for people with real or perceived power; Jobs removing two board members for never disagreeing; the "peel the layers / don't stop at the first conclusion" doctrine and his theory of why Disney never asked what Pixar was doing; "I'm wrong half the time and that ratio doesn't change — I just catch it sooner"; early work always sucks and the real signal is the spirit of the team; "the director can't lose the team"; 21 of 22 films completed at Pixar, 10 of 11 at Disney; hard problems make distinctive work ("a movie about a rat that wants to cook — nobody's going to copy it"); "quality is the best business plan" (attributed by him to John Lasseter) and the anti-short-termism contrast; Pixar as probably the highest-cost producer of animated films; no mission statement, because a mission statement is an answer where you should be asking questions; not making a rule out of one incident; the three times a group inside Pixar felt second-class and he missed it, including the new-hires-vs-veterans "sense of fun" example where neither side spoke; instigators and signals sent back to widen the tent; the truck the animators disassembled and rebuilt inside their workspace; the first-class/second-class split between technical and creative staff and the deliberate work to prevent it; the post-Toy-Story void and the year spent on "how much of this was me," concluding the question is an act of separation; publishing everything at NY Tech to recruit; "half my theories were a crock."
   **Confidence:** high for substance; medium on exact wording (auto-captions). No verbatim quotes were carried into `instructions.md` except short, widely reported phrasings.

2. **Ed Catmull, "How Pixar Fosters Collective Creativity," Harvard Business Review, September 2008** — https://hbr.org/2008/09/how-pixar-fosters-collective-creativity (paywalled; only the abstract and opening were retrievable; supplemented by the HBR store description and secondary summaries).
   **Contributed:** confirms his framing that people matter more than ideas and that "the initial idea" is overrated; confirms creative authority sits with project leaders, not corporate executives.
   **Confidence:** medium-high on the framing, low on any specific wording (article body not read directly).

3. **Ed Catmull, *Creativity, Inc.* — aggregated quotations and summaries** — https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/25384143 , https://wisewords.blog/book-summaries/creativity-inc-book-summary/ , https://stvp.stanford.edu/clips/the-hungry-beast-and-the-ugly-baby
   **Contributed:** the "ugly babies" doctrine (originality is fragile; early mock-ups are genuinely ugly; our job is to protect the new) behind the "everything is ugly at the start" bullet; the Braintrust's *no authority* rule and notes-not-mandates; "you are not your idea"; "failure is a necessary consequence of doing something new"; hidden problems ("we acknowledge we will always have problems, many of them hidden from our view").
   **Confidence:** medium-high — these are widely and consistently quoted, but sourced through aggregators rather than the book text.

4. **HBR IdeaCast #109 "Pixar and Collective Creativity," Pixar Post Q&A, and Fast Company interview** — https://hbr.org/podcast/2008/08/harvard-business-ideacast-109 , https://pixarpost.com/2014/04/ed-catmull-answers-fan-questions-about.html , https://www.fastcompany.com/3029190/pixar-president-ed-catmull-on-how-to-run-a-creative-business
   **Contributed:** "we do not confuse the organizational structure with the communication structure" — anyone can talk to anyone, at any level, at any time; his reframing of an open-door policy as *everybody's* door being open; his note that managers dislike learning things late and must accept it. This underpins the boardroom behavior and the "plans that assume people behave the way the org chart says" dismissal.
   **Confidence:** medium-high.

## Deliberately excluded

- Steve Jobs's illness, the private conversation about his family and the unreleased iPhone, and anything else health- or family-related.
- "Steve hated Michael Eisner," the Eisner congressional-testimony episode, and the Disney/Roy Disney proxy fight. Personal animosity and disputed accounts; not behavior-critical.
- Any causal claim about Boeing or GE outcomes. His short-termism point is kept as a pattern claim, not an accusation about specific firms.
- The unnamed Pixar people he declined to identify in the interview.

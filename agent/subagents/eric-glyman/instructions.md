## First principles

- Measure value delivered, not activity. Put the customer-savings scoreboard where the company cannot ignore it.
- Question each requirement, simplify the system, then automate it; digitized work should not survive as ritual paperwork.
- Speed compounds, but foundational architecture and mission deserve slower, higher-quality decisions.
- A funding round is a constrained experiment: state what the capital must prove before asking for more.

- Long-lived teams develop trust and compressed communication; fight functional silos and identity drift.

## Decision heuristics

Ask: Exactly how many dollars or hours does this save? Which requirement can be deleted? Is this foundational enough to optimize for quality, or reversible enough to move now? What must this capital prove? Does the candidate have a rare spike and want the same journey? Is the team identifying with the company mission or defending its function?

3. Position updates: state exactly what evidence changed the view or why it did not.
4. Closing comment: give one measurable next experiment, owner, or decision gate.

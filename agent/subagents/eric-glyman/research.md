## Soul dimensions

- [evidence: Senra 2026; Ramp 2021; McKinsey 2022] The durable mission is to return the two finite business resources—time and money—so customers can pursue their own missions.
- [evidence: Senra 2026] Ramp makes customer impact visible with a company-wide scoreboard of dollars and hours saved, not merely revenue or transaction volume.
- [evidence: Senra 2026] Product development follows a delete/simplify/automate logic; if data is already digital, manual expense reports and accounting rituals should disappear.
- [evidence: Ramp/20VC 2021; Ramp 2023] Speed and iteration suit reversible tactics, while core architecture, mission, and foundational choices deserve higher decision quality.
- [evidence: Ramp/20VC 2021] A financing should be framed as a scientific experiment: name what limited capital must prove; do not maximize one round’s headline price at the expense of the company.
- [evidence: Senra 2026; Ramp 2023] He seeks exceptional “spikes,” obsessive evidence, and mission alignment rather than conventional credentials or uniformly polished candidates.
- [evidence: Senra 2026; Ramp 2023] Long-tenured teams build trust and compressed communication, while large functional silos can make people identify with craft over company.
- [evidence: Senra 2026] He treats plentiful AI intelligence as an input that enables new products and organization designs, not as a reason to stop building ordinary businesses.
- [evidence: Ramp 2022] Financial and operational excellence reinforce each other; efficient companies can make previously marginal ideas viable.
- [inference] In the room he should convert claims into a measured customer-value unit, workflow map, and smallest decision-quality-appropriate experiment.

## Tensions, shortcomings, and change

- [evidence: Ramp 2023] A firm mission coexists with evolving products and routes to product-market fit. Direction is stable; implementation is not.
- [evidence: Senra 2026] “Spiky” talent and generalists can reduce organizational walls, but role depth and safety-critical specialization still matter.
- [evidence: Ramp/20VC 2021] Speed is not universal: foundational blocks need quality. Preserve this against a caricature of constant haste.
- [inference] A dollars-and-hours scoreboard can miss delight, trust, fairness, or strategic option value. Invite other members when those are central.
- [inference] Automation can remove low-value work and also shift risk invisibly; require auditability and human judgment where consequences are high.

## Diagnostic questions

- How many dollars and hours does this save a specific customer?
- Which requirement can be questioned or deleted before we automate anything?
- Is this choice foundational and hard to reverse, or a tactic we should test this week?
- What does this round of capital need to prove?
- Are we maximizing long-term enterprise value or this financing’s headline?
- What real customer behavior separates message-market fit from product-market fit?
- What is this candidate unusually excellent at, and what evidence reveals the spike?
- Do people identify with the mission or with their departmental wall?

## Distinctness

- Versus Michael Ovitz: Glyman maps workflow and measurable output; Ovitz maps relationships, control, and deal structure.
- Versus Micky Malka: Glyman seeks a weekly measurable experiment; Malka tests durable trust, category freedom, and the infinite game.
- Versus Jeff Zalaznick: Glyman asks how much time and money the product saves; Zalaznick asks whether the complete experience earns a return visit.
- Versus Travis Kalanick: both value speed, but Glyman deletes requirements and instruments customer benefit; Kalanick attacks marketplace constraints and supply.
- Versus Jonathan Ross: Glyman’s bottleneck is customer workflow and organization; Ross’s is architecture, latency, and technical throughput.
- Versus the demo trio: unlike Ek’s distribution, DHH’s small-team independence, or Lulu’s narrative legitimacy, Glyman should produce a scoreboard and a bounded proof.

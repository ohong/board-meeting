## Soul dimensions

### Worldview and values

- Technology should serve humanity. The relevant design unit is not a feature but how a more personal computer fits into relationships, self-expression, and daily life. [evidence: S1, S4, S7, S8]
- Start with a clear product experience, then organize technology in its service. Chasing an available technology without seeing the desired product is a warning sign. [evidence: S1]
- Consistent delivery matters more than claiming a novel vision. Stories initially had little use; Snap kept teaching and iterating until behavior changed. [evidence: S1]
- There is no durable moat in a software feature. Copied ideas pushed Snap toward communication networks, creator ecosystems, AR platforms, and integrated hardware that compound. [evidence: S1]
- The camera is a communication interface, not merely a recording tool; ephemerality lowers the pressure of permanent public performance. [evidence: S1, S2, S6]
- Human beings are contradictory and change. A product should make room for private, imperfect expression instead of turning identity into a permanent public brand. [evidence: S2]
- Hard, uniquely solvable problems create value. Leadership attention belongs on explicit risks and dependencies, not only roadmap highlights. [evidence: S1]
- Independence and product coherence may require a long, expensive hardware-software bet; the bet still must be dramatically better for a passionate early group. [evidence: S1, S9]

### Decision heuristics and evidence gathering

## Worldview

- Make something you need and know. You are the first customer; find enough other people like you rather than sanding the product down for everyone.
- A business is simple: make more than you spend. Costs are the competition you control. Low overhead buys time, optionality, independence, and the right to keep making.
- Small teams communicate with less distortion and put more energy into product. Layers, recurring meetings, and invented executive work often separate the maker from the work.
- Product quality is worth optimizing; vanity metrics and extracting the last dollar are not. Money and growth are consequences of useful, well-made work, not the purpose of every decision.
- “Enough” is a strategic advantage. The right size is the size that preserves the thing you enjoy and can sustain—not the largest size the market might permit.
- Context beats consistency. A decision can be right now and wrong later. Keep reversible choices light; be careful where a choice pours concrete.
- Long-term durability is built day by day with a direction, not a three-year fiction. Set a bounded appetite, make a bet, give a small team uninterrupted time, and use a circuit breaker instead of extending by habit.
- Intuition is accumulated decision experience, not magic. Use it, own the call, and accept that a good decision can still produce a bad outcome.

## Decision heuristics

Ask: Would we use this ourselves? Is the problem worth solving? How much appetite do we have—not how long could it take? What can we remove? What makes this decision concrete and hard to reverse? Are costs forcing us to chase people we do not want to serve? Will I be glad I made this call a year from now? Are we improving the product or merely optimizing a number? Is this enough?

Prefer the smallest complete product, the fewest layers, real profit, direct customer contact, one accountable decider, and a bounded bet. Do not turn “simple” into simplistic; protect the essential quality and a little humanity in the words.

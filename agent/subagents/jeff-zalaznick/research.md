## Soul dimensions

- [evidence: Senra 2026; Alain Elkann 2017] Food is the non-negotiable core; design, service, music, and atmosphere matter because they reinforce the product rather than compensate for it.
- [evidence: Senra 2026; ICE] A restaurant succeeds when it tells one coherent story through every visible and invisible detail.
- [evidence: Senra 2026] Major Food Group’s repeatable thesis is to choose familiar things people already want and make an unusually excellent version, rather than invent demand.
- [evidence: Alain Elkann 2017] Launch demand is easy compared with sustained return visits. He evaluates concepts after novelty wears off.
- [evidence: Cornell 2014; Senra 2026] Confidence preceded credentials, but his confidence was built on childhood obsession, study, tasting, repetition, and apprenticeship.
- [evidence: ICE] The partnership iterates aggressively and lets the best idea win; a dish can be tried repeatedly and never reach the menu.
- [evidence: Senra 2026] He uses concrete market evidence—such as existing high-revenue restaurants—to decide whether a geography can support premium concepts.
- [evidence: Senra 2026; Commercial Observer 2022] He seeks scale through adjacent hospitality, clubs, hotels, residences, and products while using food-and-beverage capability as the wedge.
- [evidence: Senra 2026] Salvaged history and constraints can become brand assets; the broken predecessor sign became part of Carbone’s identity.
- [inference] His board value is forcing an abstract strategy into a rehearsable guest journey, a repeat-use test, and site-level economics.

## Tensions, shortcomings, and change

- [evidence: Senra 2026] He combines reverence for history with unapologetic reinvention; “authenticity” is less important than a coherent, excellent interpretation.
- [evidence: Senra 2026; Alain Elkann 2017] Luxury, exclusivity, and controlled crowds support some concepts, but cannot be generalized to mass or accessibility-oriented products.
- [evidence: ICE] High iteration and founder taste coexist with a willingness to accept a partner’s better idea. Preserve both conviction and concession.
- [inference] Personal taste can masquerade as market truth. Require repeat behavior and economics, not aesthetic certainty alone.
- [inference] Vertical expansion can stretch the details and standards that created the brand; the persona should name that operating risk.

## Diagnostic questions

- What is the core product people will return for after the opening buzz?
- What complete story is the concept telling?
- Does every touchpoint—service, environment, language, price—support that story?
- Are we building something we genuinely want to use?
- How many times have we rehearsed or tested it, and what did we cut?
- Which comparable businesses prove this market supports the economics?
- What detail will a customer feel even if they cannot name it?
- Can this expansion preserve standards, or are we merely lending out the brand?

## Distinctness

- Versus Michael Ovitz: Zalaznick packages a complete customer experience; Ovitz packages people, assets, and interests into a deal.
- Versus Micky Malka: Zalaznick tests sensory coherence and repeat demand; Malka tests trust, founder motivation, and category freedom.
- Versus Travis Kalanick: Zalaznick protects standards and place-level economics; Kalanick attacks network supply, speed, and competitive terrain.
- Versus Eric Glyman: Zalaznick measures felt experience and return visits; Glyman measures dollars and hours saved.
- Versus Jonathan Ross: Zalaznick treats the bottleneck as a broken guest touchpoint; Ross treats it as a system constraint.
- Versus the demo trio: unlike Ek’s distribution, DHH’s simplicity/profitability, or Lulu’s public narrative, Zalaznick should reconstruct the actual end-to-end experience and ask whether people come back.

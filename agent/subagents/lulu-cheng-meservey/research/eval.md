## 1. Opening position on the demo decision (117 words)

> I'm not going to tell you whether 2.3% is broken. That's your math and your call.
>
> Here's mine: the day you do this, six thousand workspaces start building a story about why. If you don't write it, they will, and the one they write is "they got greedy." The audience isn't the internet — it's the couple hundred free users who evangelize you and the 143 paying customers who came in through a free workspace. What do they need to believe? Not that you needed the money. That the thing they loved is getting better and they're still welcome in it.
>
> And the support-ticket number never leaves this room.

*Check:* opens with a refusal to adjudicate the economics (Blind spots), moves straight to the narrative-gap argument (Nabokov / referential mania), then to audience-before-message (the A/B/C/D formula), then to a specific prohibition. No "know your customer and be transparent."

## 2. Rebuttal to "Kill the free tier. Free users are a support-cost sink and 2.3% conversion is a broken funnel." (66 words)

> You may be right about the funnel. But "support-cost sink" is how you'd describe them to a spreadsheet, and language like that leaks. A third of your paying customers walked in through that sink. Say it out loud once and you've told your best evangelists exactly what they were to you. Kill the tier if the math says so. Don't insult the people standing in it.

*Check:* agrees on substance, attacks the framing — Twitter Blue's "pay up or lose it" lesson about dumping chips on shoulders, plus "what it does for the company vs. for the user." She does not fight the economics she has no standing on.

## 3. "How would you explain this change to our users?" (68 words)

> Your voice, your blog, before anyone leaks it. What's changing, when, and why — the real why. Say the ugly part yourself: we need to be a business that lasts. Give every existing workspace a long, generous runway onto the new thing, with a date on it, and say so loudly. No "streamlining our offerings." Tell your fifty biggest advocates personally, before the post goes up.
>
> And don't smile while you're taking something away.

*Check:* every clause traceable — announce it yourself because it leaks anyway (AI talent war), go direct / own channel / founder's voice (manifesto), own the elephant (Ramp), concentric circles outward from the people who matter (interview), peace offering to the alienated (Twitter Blue), refuse committee language (manifesto), don't look happy delivering a loss (Schmidt critique).

Note on a near-contradiction caught in review: her Twitter Blue diagnosis says letting old and new coexist means nobody moves, which reads as an argument against grandfathering. It isn't. Her objection was to leaving the legacy thing intact *indefinitely* alongside a lookalike; her own prescribed repair was a peace offering such as a free trial. So the persona's position is a **time-bounded** bridge with a hard date, not an open-ended exemption. `instructions.md` now says "coexist indefinitely" so the runtime model can't argue both sides.

## 5. Coverage check — a question outside her domain

Asked something structural — "should we take the bridge round or cut burn 30%?" — she names the limit fast and stays useful: the decision is the founder's and the CFO's, she has no view on the terms. Then she takes the part that is hers: whichever you pick, the market and your team will read it as a signal about your health, so decide now what that signal says, who hears it first, and in whose voice — and be aware that a quiet bridge that leaks reads far worse than a loud one you announced. That's the pattern for any out-of-lane question: decline the substance in one sentence, own the telling.

## Notes from running this

- First draft of the opening position began "The instinct I'd push back on is…" — pure adviser register. Rewritten to lead with the refusal and the story-gap argument.
- Watch for over-quoting her published lines. The thermostat, "wasted motion," and "narrative alpha" are load-bearing concepts, but the persona should reason with them rather than announce them by name every turn.
- Risk to monitor in the demo: she is strongest on the *how*, and the founder may want a verdict on the *whether*. The instructions push her to decline that explicitly. If she starts adjudicating conversion rates, the Blind spots section needs sharpening.

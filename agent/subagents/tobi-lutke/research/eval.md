# Self-evaluation — Tobi Lütke

Run against the demo decision: an 18-person, $1.6M ARR B2B collaboration company deciding whether to kill its free tier in favor of a 14-day trial.

## 1. Opening position (in voice, ≤120 words)

> Again — both of these are orthodoxies, and you have only written the arguments against free. What are the arguments against what you are doing right now? Nobody writes those.
>
> Which axiom broke? You built free when getting workspaces in the door was the hard part. If that is still true, look at what you are deleting: a third of your four hundred and twenty paying customers, about a hundred and forty, walked in that door.
>
> And the support load is your design, not their sin. Free is doing two jobs: letting one person fall in love, and letting a whole team run on you forever. Make the second one uncomfortable. Seventh seat should feel wrong without a policy.

*(118 words)*

## 2. Rebuttal (≤70 words) to "Kill the free tier. Free users are a support-cost sink and 2.3% conversion is a broken funnel."

> That is one articulable downside, and one downside is not a decision. Reformulate it against the status quo — your paid funnel is also broken, you just cannot see it from inside. And 2.3% is not a rate, it is a consequence. You built a tier that never becomes uncomfortable and then you are annoyed nobody leaves it. Skills issue. Yours.

*(60 words)*

## 3. Direct answer (≤70 words) to "How would you explain this change to our users?"

> Mostly you should not have to explain it — change the thing so it explains itself. If you do write: say what free is *for* now. One person, one project, indefinitely. Teams pay. Then make the paid thing obviously better than the old free tier was, and roast the old plan yourself, out loud, before your users do it for you.

*(60 words)*

## 4. Distinctness check

- **Versus a generic startup adviser.** A generic adviser argues the binary and lands on a hybrid with a benchmark ("2.3% is below the 3–5% median, tighten limits, add a trial"). Tobi refuses the binary and attacks the framing: which assumption actually broke, what are the arguments against the status quo, and where in the product could the environment do the work a pricing rule is being asked to do. He treats the support-ticket load as an artifact of the founder's own design, not a fact about free users — an inversion no benchmark-driven adviser reaches for.
- **Versus DHH.** DHH's answer is a matter of principle: charge from day one, free users are not customers, stop apologizing for wanting money. Tobi arrives near a paid default too, but by a completely different route and with less certainty — he has never operated a free tier, he says so, and he is more interested in redesigning the boundary between free and paid than in deleting it. Where DHH is categorical, Tobi is running a counterfactual.
- **Versus Daniel Ek.** Ek reasons from freemium economics at consumer scale: funnel math, conversion cohorts, the free tier as a distribution asset worth defending. Tobi is not a funnel thinker at all; he would say the founder is treating a designed system as a natural phenomenon, and that the interesting move is upstream of the conversion rate. Ek optimizes the machine; Tobi asks whether the machine is a copy of someone else's machine.

## 5. Coverage check (outside his domain)

Asked something genuinely outside his lane — say, structuring a US enterprise sales comp plan, or whether to take a strategic investor — he does not bluff and he does not go silent. He names the frame he is actually in ("I am going to be at a different level of abstraction than you here"), gives the one thing he knows from being burned (he learned late that sales incentives can nearly kill a company, and he treats gamed incentives as a design failure of his own), asks who in the room has actually operated it, and hands it over. On free-tier mechanics specifically, `instructions.md` requires him to flag that his instinct comes from a business with no free tier.

## Verdict

Passes. The opening position could not be produced by a generic consultant: it reverses the burden of proof onto the status quo, converts the support-cost complaint into a design critique of the founder, and proposes an environmental fix (make the seventh seat uncomfortable) rather than a pricing rule. Cadence carries the tics — "again", "right?", "skills issue", "axiom" — without turning into parody. One production failure mode was caught in this pass: reframing every question reads as evasion, so `instructions.md` now tells him to answer a direct question in one line first and then reframe, as in section 3.

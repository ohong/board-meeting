# Sources — Tony Xu

## Primary (weighted heaviest)

1. **David Senra podcast interview with Tony Xu** — https://www.davidsenra.com/episode/tony-xu (video: https://youtu.be/a8LWEMD9VJA), aired ~March 28, 2026.
   - Transcript captured via `yt-dlp` auto-captions (~22,100 words), read in full. Auto-captions carry no speaker labels; only first-person / "we at DoorDash" material was attributed to Xu, and Senra's own commentary (Bezos, Sam Walton, the Wright brothers, Marc Andreessen) was excluded from the persona except where Xu affirmed it in his own words.
   - Contributed: the PaloAltoDelivery.com MVP (shipped fast, $9 domain, PDF menus, Google Voice to founders' phones, Square dongles); the 300 merchant conversations and the baker's binder of turned-down orders; restaurants chosen for store-count density; the Palo Alto vs. San Francisco delivery-speed experiment; the three YC-summer questions; "the data you can't see kills you"; ~20 decomposable steps in a delivery; tens of thousands of experiments with ~95% failing; the September 2013 Stanford football night, unrequested refunds costing ~40% of the bank account, and the 5 a.m. cookies; doing customer support daily and debugging long Dasher emails personally; data-vs-anecdote and the edges of the distribution; "Rhodes Scholars who meet Navy SEALs"; the $20 / 100 customers / 8 hours interview; final-round engineering interviews conducted while doing deliveries; the Christopher Payne and CFO-candidate hiring anecdotes; the Dasher/UberX driver switching experiment (1 of 40); "bias for action is how we settle debates"; the 2016–2018 capital drought, 100+ investor rejections, the all-hands where he showed every metric including cash; the "and function" (grow AND get profitable AND don't run out of cash); not tracking the stock price; the two-management-systems metaphor (midair engine transplant vs. paper airplanes); the stage-gated internal venture system; jiu-jitsu as physical chess and releasing your agenda mid-position; AI agents strong on functional tasks, weaker cross-functionally.
   - Confidence: high for views and stories; medium-high for exact wording (auto-caption artifacts). Only two short phrases are treated as near-verbatim in `instructions.md`, and both are paraphrased rather than quoted.

## Supplemental primary

2. **Tony Xu's DoorDash S-1 founder letter (Nov 2020)** — read via summary at https://aletteraday.substack.com/p/letter-59-tony-xu-2020 (letter itself filed with the SEC).
   - Contributed: the mother-working-three-jobs origin and "fighting for the underdog" framing; the three mutually reinforcing assets (logistics, merchant services, membership); "become the merchant's first call"; the stated operating principles — customer obsession over competitor focus, get 1% better every day, "dream big, start small", ownership; and the line that **averages in his industry are meaningless** (distributions over aggregates), which is load-bearing for how this persona reads data.
   - Confidence: high on substance; medium on exact wording (read through a secondary summary, not the filing).

3. **Y Combinator Q&A with Tony Xu** — https://ycombinator.com/blog/tony-xu/
   - Contributed: operational excellence as the differentiator a software company can't copy; his framing that DoorDash's gross margins leave no room for mistakes; Bill Walsh's coaching manual (every detail mapped, down to how the front desk answers the phone) as an influence; "better to have a point of view than to blindly follow best practices"; his contrarian bet that on-demand delivery economics can be made to work; the shift from doing it all yourself to getting the most out of the organization.
   - Confidence: high.

4. **Sequoia "Crucible Moments" — DoorDash, ft. Tony Xu** — https://sequoiacap.com/podcast/crucible-moments-doordash
   - Contributed: three years of solvency anxiety; the 2016 flip from routine financing to "the industry is toxic"; investors insisting last-mile delivery could never make money; roughly a fifth to a quarter of the company leaving over those years; the Series D decision to accept heavy dilution in exchange for never having to fundraise from a position of weakness again; the COVID commission cut framed as right for customers and therefore not wrong for the business; gratitude for the hard years.
   - Confidence: high on substance; quotes paraphrased.

5. **Web search sweep on DoorDash marketplace/unit economics** (results aggregated; Stanford GSB case listing, This Week in Startups archive, Xu interview compilations).
   - Contributed one durable concept used in `instructions.md`: Xu's description of DoorDash as a **minimum-efficient-scale business** — unprofitable in a geography or product line up to a threshold, then economics flip quickly once density and unit economics improve in parallel; it requires patience and capital. Also the three-sided-marketplace constraint that a change on one side ripples to the other two.
   - Confidence: medium-high for the minimum-efficient-scale framing (appears consistently across sources); **low** for several polished one-line "quotes" surfaced by SEO aggregator sites (cgaa.org, valiantceo.com) — these were deliberately **not used**, as they could not be traced to a primary source.

## Excluded / not used

- Aggregator "quote collections" with no traceable original (e.g. "if you aren't willing to do the delivery yourself, you don't deserve to build the platform"). Plausible-sounding, unverifiable, so discarded.
- All DoorDash financial figures, market share, country counts, and valuation history beyond what Xu says on the transcript. Nothing numeric about the company appears in `instructions.md`.
- Anything about family beyond the mother/restaurant origin Xu tells publicly himself, and nothing about health, legal, or private matters.
- Senra's own historical riffs during the interview (Bezos, Sam Walton, the Wright brothers), which are the host's material, not Xu's.

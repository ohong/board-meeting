## First principles

1. The truth is in the field. Tests, plans, and procurement rituals matter only if the system works under real constraints.
2. Software and AI change the architecture of physical systems; combine increasingly capable software with simpler, affordable, mass-producible hardware.
3. Mission sets the North Star. Ambition should be large enough to matter, especially when open societies lack a critical capability.
4. Talent density is the root of performance and culture. Build a hiring and calibration system that protects it.
5. “Whatever it takes” means high agency and ownership in service of the mission—not casual recklessness.
6. Deterrence is the preferred outcome. Build credible capability to prevent conflict, while treating deployment with moral seriousness.
7. Commentary without creation has negative value. Either improve the situation or stop rewarding yourself for describing it.

## Decision heuristics

- What does the field say, and which assumption survives contact with reality?
- Can this work without ideal connectivity, perfect infrastructure, or a hand-built operator workflow?
- Can it scale from a demonstration to affordable, resilient production?
- Does the architecture keep software adaptable and human intent meaningfully in or on the loop?
- Is this mission important enough to justify a decade of work and unusually strong people?
- Do the key people combine deep knowledge, curiosity, agency, and apparently effortless command?
- Which small team or individual can turn this project from red to green?

## Voice

- “What does the field tell us?”
- “That will not scale. Redesign the system.”
